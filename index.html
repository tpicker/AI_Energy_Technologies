TECHNOLOGY 01 / Fossil fuels

Natural Gas Combined Cycle

200–800 MW

Range shown in: Student resource packet. Packet page 2.

Explore the supplied background, examples, diagrams, and video resources. External links open in a new tab.

Background

Natural gas combined-cycle plants in the 200–800 MW range align closely with the power requirements being discussed for many hyperscale AI campuses. These facilities offer:

  • High efficiency (typically 55–64%)
  • Continuous dispatchable generation
  • Lower emissions than coal generation
  • Fast ramping capability to support variable loads
  • Proven utility-scale operation adjacent to major transmission networks

This size range is particularly relevant because many announced AI data center projects are targeting approximately 200–800 MW of dedicated power demand, making these facilities useful benchmarks for energy infrastructure discussions.

TECHNOLOGY 02 / Fossil fuels

Natural Gas Peaker

50–200 MW

Range shown in: Student resource packet. Packet page 3.

Explore the supplied background, examples, diagrams, and video resources. External links open in a new tab.

Background

Natural gas peaker plants differ from combined-cycle plants because they are designed for rapid startup (minutes rather than hours) and are often proposed as backup or supplemental generation for AI data centers when electricity demand spikes. They typically:

  • Have lower capital cost.
  • Start quickly.
  • Operate fewer hours per year.
  • Have lower efficiency than combined-cycle plants.
  • Provide grid reliability during peak demand and emergency conditions.

TECHNOLOGY 04 / Nuclear

Large Nuclear Plant

1,000–1,600 MW

Range shown in: Student resource packet. Packet page 5.

Explore the supplied background, examples, diagrams, and video resources. External links open in a new tab.

Background

Large nuclear generating stations in the 1,000–1,600 MW range are increasingly being discussed as long-term power sources for hyperscale AI infrastructure because they provide:

  • 24/7 carbon-free baseload generation
  • Capacity factors often exceeding 90%
  • Stable power output independent of weather
  • Large single-site generation suitable for gigawatt-scale AI campuses
  • Long operating lifetimes (40–80 years)

This size range closely aligns with the power requirements being discussed for the largest future AI campuses, where demand may exceed 1 GW of continuous electricity consumption.

Small Modular Reactors

50–300 MW

Range shown in: Student resource packet. Packet page 6.

Explore the supplied background, examples, diagrams, and video resources. External links open in a new tab.

Background

Small Modular Reactors (SMRs) in the 50–300 MW range are increasingly being proposed as dedicated power sources for AI campuses and hyperscale data centers because they offer:

  • 24/7 carbon-free generation
  • Modular deployment close to large loads
  • Smaller footprints than traditional nuclear plants
  • High reliability and capacity factors
  • Ability to add capacity incrementally as AI demand grows
  • Reduced transmission requirements when co-located with data centers

The 50–300 MW range aligns particularly well with individual AI campuses, regional data center clusters, and dedicated behind-the-meter power strategies being evaluated by major technology companies.

Solar Plus Storage

100–500 MW

Range shown in: Student resource packet. Packet page 8.

Explore the supplied background, examples, diagrams, and video resources. External links open in a new tab.

Background

Solar + Storage projects in the 100–500 MW solar range are increasingly being evaluated for AI data centers because they provide:

  • Carbon-free daytime electricity generation

Biomass and Waste to Energy

20–100 MW · Student resource packet · Packet page 13

Biomass and Waste-to-Energy facilities in the 20–100 MW range are increasingly evaluated as supplemental power sources for AI data centers because they provide:

  • Dispatchable generation available 24/7
  • Utilization of waste streams that would otherwise go to landfills
  • High-capacity factors compared with solar and wind
  • Potential reduction in methane emissions from landfilled waste
  • Localized energy production near urban load centers
  • Compatibility with district energy and industrial heat recovery systems

The 20–100 MW range is particularly relevant for regional AI campuses, edge computing facilities, and distributed data center clusters seeking firm generation with a smaller footprint than traditional fossil-fuel plants.

Grid Scale Battery Storage

50–300 MW · Student resource packet · Packet page 14

Grid-scale battery storage systems in the 50–300 MW range are increasingly important for AI data centers because they provide:

  • Instantaneous response to power fluctuations
  • Backup power and resilience for critical computing loads
  • Peak shaving and demand charge reduction
  • Integration with solar and wind resources
  • Reduced dependence on diesel backup generation
  • Enhanced grid reliability and power quality

The 50–300 MW range aligns closely with the needs of individual hyperscale AI campuses and regional data center clusters, especially when paired with renewable energy, natural gas generation, or utility grid interconnections.
